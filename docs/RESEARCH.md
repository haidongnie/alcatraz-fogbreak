# Alcatraz research notes (June 1962)

Compiled by research agents from public sources (listed per section) for this game. Facts marked [est.] are estimates.

# Alcatraz, June 1962: the escape, routine and security

## 1. Escapees and cells
- All four cells were on the **ground tier ("flats") of outer B Block**. B Block had three tiers, and the cells backed onto a central utility corridor. A June 1962 press compilation says "first tier"; one weak blog says second tier, and I'd discount it.
  - **Frank Morris, cell B-138.** Moved there 30 Jan 1960. Inmate number AZ-1441.
  - **Allen West, cell B-140**, next to Morris.
  - **John Anglin, B-150, and Clarence Anglin, B-152.** The Seattle Times gives "cells 150 and 152" and Lost New England gives 152 for Clarence, so 150 is John's by elimination. The Anglins' cells were about 4 cells from Morris.
- Jim Albright, a former officer, places the cells "off Seedy Avenue (outside of B Block)", not off Broadway as the 1979 film shows.
- **Cell size:** about 9×5×7 ft, or 2.7×1.5×2.1 m.
- **Original plot:** reportedly 9 men; 5 backed out.

## 2. Digging (about Dec 1961 to June 1962)
- **Vent:** a grille in the rear wall below the sink. The original opening was about **6×10 in (15×25 cm)**.
  - Enlarged to **10½×14 in (27×36 cm)** for Morris and about **14×12 in (36×30 cm)** for the Anglins.
  - Wall: about **8 in (20 cm)** of salt-damaged concrete. West's 1962 account says 4 in.
- **Tools:**
  - Mess-hall spoons, sharpened, and later knives. One account mentions a spoon chisel reinforced with coin metal.
  - Scavenged saw blades.
  - A **drill built from a vacuum-cleaner motor**. The 1962 press said an electric fan motor.
  - A crowbar, a crude flashlight and a periscope for watching for guards.
- **Noise cover:** "music hour" in the early evening, with Morris playing accordion.
- **Hiding the work:** fake grilles of cardboard or canvas board painted to look like metal. The NPS holds one: **25×16×9 cm, canvas board, soap, paint, metal**. Accordion cases and towels were also placed in front.
- **Dummy heads:** papier-mâché-like mix of **soap, toothpaste, concrete dust and toilet paper**. Painted flesh tone with paint from the maintenance shop or art kits, with **real hair from the barbershop floor**. Towels and clothing went under the blankets to form a body. The 1962 press said the hair was "brush-bristle".

## 3. Workshop on top of B Block
- A wire-mesh enclosure around the **ventilation machinery on top of B Block**, below the cellhouse roof. It was hidden with blankets.
- B Block's fan motor had been removed in 1957 and never replaced. That left the shaft open above the workshop.
- The elevated gun walk could see the area by day, but per 1962 reports it was **not manned after 9:30 pm lights-out**.
- West claimed guards let him work unattended painting the top of the block.
- **Raft:** **more than 50 rubberized cotton raincoats** (WWII-era issue).
  - Size: **6×14 ft, about 1.8×4.3 m**.
  - Seams hand-stitched and sealed with contact cement, then cured on the hot steam pipes.
  - Some raincoats were donated by other inmates, some stolen.
- **Life vests:** raincoat material, following the **March 1962 *Popular Mechanics*** design. West found 3 left behind.
- **Paddles:** plywood and screws.
- **Pump:** a **concertina stolen from another inmate** and used as a bellows. Its keys had been removed.
- **Left behind:** an unfinished pontoon and part of a second raft for West, a paddle, 3 vests and West's dummy head.

## 4. The route, night of Monday 11 June 1962
1. **About 21:30:** the last count and lights-out. Cells had been locked since late afternoon: 16:50 per the regulations, 17:30 per the 1962 press.
2. The three men squeezed through their vent holes into the **utility corridor**. It was about **3 ft (0.9 m) wide**, ran the full height of the block, was unlit and was unpatrolled. Clarence kicked at West's grille from the corridor side, but it would not give.
3. They climbed the plumbing, drain pipes and walkways **3 tiers, about 30 ft (9 m)**, to the top of the block.
4. **Up the ventilator shaft:** they had already removed its **rivets** with a crude wrench and replaced them with a fake bolt made of soap. They pried off one bar and **bent a ½-in (13 mm) steel crossbar**.
   - Diameter: "no more than 12 in" (30 cm) in one report; another gives a **14-in (36 cm) shaft and 20-in (51 cm) pipe**. My best estimate is a 36–50 cm opening.
   - A 1962 photo caption places this exit vent at the **east end of B Block**.
5. **Shortly after 21:30:** a guard heard a crash, probably the ventilator cap hitting the roof. A lieutenant checked the hospital area and found nothing. Another officer reportedly heard footsteps overhead.
6. They crossed about **100 ft (30 m) of roof** in view of a gun tower and slid down the **kitchen/bakery vent pipe**, about **50 ft (15 m)**, at the rear of the cellhouse. They landed near the exit of the inmate shower area.
7. They climbed **two 12-ft (3.7 m) barbed-wire fences**, then passed a walkway near the water tower, a narrow road and a lumber storage area.
8. **Launch point:** the **northeast shore**, in a searchlight blind spot. The **power house hid them from the gun tower**.
   - Launch time: about 22:00 per Wikipedia; 22:30 at the earliest given the ebb, per the FBI investigator.
   - A 2014 Deltares current model suggests 23:30–24:00 was the window most likely to reach land.
   - Conditions: water about **54°F (12°C)**, tidal current about **2 mph (0.9 m/s)**.
   - West's account of the plan: Angel Island, about 2 km away, then swim Raccoon Strait to Marin, then steal a car and clothes.
9. **West:** he cleared his grille hours later and reached the roof, startling the gulls. The others were gone, so he returned to his cell before dawn.
10. **Discovery:** the **morning count on 12 June, about 07:00–07:15**. Officers Lawrence Bartlett and Bill Long reached in to wake Clarence and his dummy head rolled onto the floor. One account gives 06:00.
    - Charles H. Herman Jr. was cellhouse officer in charge on the evening of 11 June. The warden was Olin Blackwell [from memory, not checked against a source].
11. **Found afterwards:**
    - 14 Jun: a paddle about 180 m off Angel Island, and a rubber-wrapped packet of Anglin letters, addresses and photos.
    - 21 Jun: a life vest at Cronkhite Beach.
    - 22 Jun: a life vest about 46 m off Alcatraz, strings still knotted.
    - The FBI closed its case in 1979 with the men "presumed drowned". The US Marshals keep the case open.

## 5. Daily schedule (weekdays, per the 1956 regulations; 1962 times may differ by ±15 min)
| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 06:30 | Wake bell; standing count at cell fronts |
| 06:55 | March to breakfast (eaten in silence; ~20 min to eat is often cited) |
| 07:30 | Work call: march down to industries (laundry, tailor, glove/brush shops, model shop) |
| 11:20 | Lunch, then about 30 min locked in cell |
| ~12:00 | Back to work |
| 16:15 | Return from work |
| 16:25 | Dinner |
| 16:50 | Lock-up count; evening in cell (reading, music hour, quiet talk) |
| 21:30 | Lights out and last formal count |

- **Weekends and holidays:** recreation yard, up to 5 hours. Softball, handball, chess, checkers, dominoes. The yard has a 25-ft (7.6 m) wall/fence.
- **Movies:** occasional weekend films.
- **Shaving:** in cells, 3 times a week. **Showers:** 2 a week.

## 6. Counts and night security
- **Count frequency:** 13 counts a day per Wikipedia; 12 formal counts per American Heritage.
- **Day counts:** before meals, at the shops, at the yard gate and at lock-up.
- **Night:** 1962 reports say **hourly checks** by a walking officer with subdued lighting. A weak source names a 01:30 count.
- **Suggested night schedule for the game [est.]:** 21:30 formal count, then quick checks at 22:30, 23:30, 00:30, 01:30, 02:30, 03:30, 04:30 and 05:30, with a formal count at 06:30.
- **Night staff:** Wikipedia says 6 officers patrolled the 4 blocks after lock-up. My estimate for the cellhouse midnight–08:00 shift is 1 lieutenant plus 2–4 officers, with tower, patrol and control-centre posts on top [est.].
- **Gun galleries:** West and East gun galleries are caged catwalks at the ends of the cellhouse. Armed officers there were the only armed staff inside; cellhouse floor officers carried no guns.
  - By night, sources say the gallery officer "retired" or the post was unmanned. I'd use a dark, empty gallery with an occasional patrol.
- **Staffing overall:** about **1 officer per 3 inmates** (other federal prisons about 1:12). 1962 reports give **155 staff/guards**.
- **Inmate count, June 1962:** about **264** (reports range 260–280). The 1935–60 average was 263.
- **Contraband and searches:**
  - Anything not issued or authorized was contraband: money, drugs, alcohol, weapons and tools.
  - **Metal detectors** on the routes to the dining hall and industries.
  - Surprise cell shakedowns.
  - Cell property had to be laid out exactly as in the diagram in the regulations booklet.
  - No newspapers; magazines had crime articles cut out.
  - Rights were limited to food, clothing, shelter and medical care; everything else was a privilege.
- **Punishment:**
  - Loss of privileges or good time.
  - **D Block "Treatment Unit"** for segregation.
  - **"The Hole," cells D-9 to D-14** on the ground floor: 5 dark cells plus the **strip cell ("the Oriental")**, which had only a floor-drain toilet flushed from outside. Inmates there were often stripped and fed a restricted diet for 1–2 days.
  - Hole stays ran 3–19 days, with 1 shower and 1 hour of yard a week. Mattress removed by day; low-wattage bulb that guards switched off.

## 7. Uniforms
- **Inmates (Regulation 32):**
  - **Blue chambray shirt**, buttoned except the collar. There was no single standard pattern; the shirts were government stock also supplied to the Navy.
  - Trousers: grey per Wikipedia, with blue denim common later.
  - Webbed belt, work shoes, cotton long johns, blue handkerchief.
  - Outdoors: denim jacket or navy-style pea coat [est., from photos].
  - **No caps inside the cellhouse.**
  - Numbers took the form AZ-####. They were probably marked on clothing as laundry IDs rather than large chest patches [est.].
- **Officers, 1962:**
  - Peaked service cap with a metal badge. The NPS holds Herman's cap: wool, leather, nickel.
  - Dark wool jacket and trousers, light shirt, dark tie. Most photos are black-and-white, so the colour is uncertain; I'd use **dark navy or charcoal grey** [est.].

## 8. Atmosphere
- **Sound:**
  - Foghorns, including the island's own fog signals at its north and south ends.
  - Western gulls, which nest on the island; West startled them on the roof.
  - Wind and surf.
  - Clanging cell-door locking bars and echoes in the cellhouse.
  - At night: coughing, snoring, men talking in their sleep.
  - Instruments during music hour.
  - On New Year's Eve, parties and boat horns carried across the water from San Francisco, best heard from the second tier.
- **Smell:**
  - Salt and cold damp.
  - The **Ghirardelli chocolate factory** on the right breeze (Albright).
  - Steam pipes, kitchen and bakery, disinfectant, bird guano [est.].
- **Building condition:** cracked concrete, rusted catwalks and corroded handrails. Officials estimated $5 million to repair it. The prison closed 21 March 1963.

# Alcatraz Main Cellhouse, June 1962: layout, dimensions and finishes

**Method.** Most dimensions below come from the Bureau of Prisons plan "Administration Bldg. and Main Cell House, First Floor Plan" (with Section A-A). I measured it in pixels against its own 0–50 ft scale bar, which works out to about 1.72 px per foot. As a check, the plan's cell spacing comes out at about 5.6 ft, which fits 5-ft cells plus partition walls. It also shows 28–29 cells per side in B and C blocks and 14 in D, which matches the published cell counts. Plan-derived figures are marked **(plan)** and are good to about ±0.5 m.

## 1. Building envelope and orientation
- **Overall length**, from the kitchen's west wall to the administration wing's east wall: about **105 m (345 ft)** (plan). Wikipedia gives "500 ft (150 m)", but that length can't be reconciled with the 5-ft cells drawn on the plan. Use about 105 m.
- **Cellhouse hall alone:** about **65.5 m long (215 ft)**.
  - It is not a plain rectangle. The A-block side bulges out along the eastern two-thirds of the north side, and the D-block side bulges out along the western three-quarters of the south side.
  - Width is about **50 m (164 ft)** at its widest and about 39 m (128 ft) at each end.
- **Administration wing** (east end): about **15 × 36 m** (50 × 119 ft).
- **Dining/kitchen wing** (west end): the mess hall is about **17 × 21 m** (55 × 68 ft) and the kitchen about **7 × 13 m** (24 × 44 ft), both from the plan. The hospital is on the floor above.
- **Orientation:** the long axis follows the island's ridge, roughly **WNW–ESE (about 300°/120°, ±15°)**. This is my estimate from the island's geography; no source states a bearing.
  - The "east" end is the administration wing and main entrance, facing the lighthouse and the Warden's House.
  - The "west" end is the dining hall and kitchen, toward the Golden Gate.
  - A-block runs along the north/NE side. D-block and the recreation yard are on the south/SW side.
- **Construction:** reinforced concrete, built 1909–12 on top of the 1859 Citadel, whose lower floor survives as the basement. It is three storeys. Cells never touch an outside wall, so open aisles separate the cells from the large barred windows.
- **Height:** tier floors are at about **0 m, 2.5 m and 5.1 m**, and the tops of the blocks at about **7.4 m**. The interior ceiling is about **9.5 m** (plan section).
  - This agrees with the escape accounts: they climbed "about 30 ft / 9 m" of pipes to reach the ceiling ventilator.
  - Outside, the roof is about **15 m** above grade at the west/north end. The escapees slid down a 50-ft (15 m) pipe there; the ground falls away on that side.

## 2. Cell blocks (all three tiers high)

| Block | Layout | Cells | Approx. block size (plan) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (north) | Double-sided, original 1912 cells: flat strap-iron lattice, key locks, spiral stairs. In 1962 used for storage, a law library and a barber shop at its end | Plan shows about 22–24 per side per tier. No source gives a count | 44 m long × 7 m deep |
| B (centre-north) | Double-sided, back to back with a utility corridor between | **28 per side per tier = 168** | 53 m × 6.9 m × 7.4 m high |
| C (centre-south) | Same as B. The two end cells were converted to guard toilets | **168** | 53 m × 6.9 m |
| D (south, "Treatment Unit") | Single-sided. The cells back onto a concrete wall facing C-block and open toward the south windows | **42 = 14 per tier × 3** (36 segregation + 6 solitary). Cells 9–14 are "The Hole", on the ground tier at the east end; the last one is the Strip Cell | About 31 m long; cell spacing about 2.1 m, so wider than B/C cells |

- B and C together have **336 cells**. There were 348 originally; 12 were removed to fit stairs at the block ends.
- **Standard cell:** **2.74 m deep × 1.52 m wide × 2.13 m high** (9 × 5 × 7 ft). Britannica Kids gives 10 × 4.5 ft instead.
  - Furniture: bunk, fold-down table and seat, two shelves, and a sink and toilet on the back wall.
  - Below the sink is a vent grille of **15 × 23 cm (6 × 9 in)** opening into the utility corridor.
- **Doors (B/C/D):** 1934 tool-proof steel fronts by Stewart Iron Works: vertical round bars with flat horizontal bands and a sliding door. A lever "gang-lock" box at the end of each tier opens one door or all of them.
- **D-block solitary cells:** an inner barred door plus an outer solid steel door.
- **Tier walkways:** about **1.2 m (4 ft)** wide, cantilevered off the 2nd and 3rd tiers, with pipe railings. Stairs are at the block ends.
- **Utility corridor (pipe chase):** runs the full length and height of A, B and C blocks between the back-to-back cells.
  - Clear width is **about 0.9 m (3 ft)** (Wikipedia). The plan shows about 1.5 m between the wall lines.
  - It is packed with plumbing, electrical and vent pipes and is open up to the roof ventilator.

## 3. Corridors (measured from cell face to cell face or wall, plan)
- **Michigan Avenue:** between A and B, about **4.2 m (14 ft)**.
- **Broadway:** between B and C, the main aisle, about **5.1 m (17 ft)**. The upper walkways narrow it to about 2.7 m overhead.
- **Times Square:** the open area at the west end of Broadway in front of the dining-hall door, under a large wall clock.
- **Park Avenue:** along the south face of C-block toward the library, about **4.2 m**.
- **"Seedy Street":** I found no source that uses this name. If you need it, the best guess is the C/D aisle (a pun on "C-D") as an informal alias of Park Avenue.
- **Sunset Strip:** the D-block aisle between the cell fronts and the south windows, about **4.6 m**.
- **North aisle** outside A-block: about **4.0 m**. One secondary source calls an A-block-side area "Sunrise Alley".

## 4. Rooms and security features
- **Administration wing** (east end), from the plan legend:
  - (1) Sally port with metal detectors: the main entrance, then barred double gates into the east end of the cellhouse. The "cutoff" was presumably this barred vestibule; no source defines it.
  - (2) Armory.
  - (3) Visiting room, in the SE corner against the cellhouse wall. Inmates and visitors spoke by phone through thick glass.
  - The control room (updated in 1961) and the warden's office are also in this wing. No source gives exact room positions.
- **Gun galleries:** one at the **west end** and one at the **east end**, each spanning the full width of the hall. The west gallery also runs into D-block (Coy entered D-block from it in 1946).
  - They are barred steel cages; in 1946 the west one had no mesh. Each has two walkways at roughly the 2nd and 3rd tier levels (about 2.5 m and 5 m). The two-level heights are my estimate.
  - Armed officers reached them from outside the inmate area. Only the gallery and tower officers carried weapons.
- **Library:** at the east end of the C/D aisle, beside the east end of D-block, about **13 × 10 m** (plan; the plan's label reads "9", probably a misprint for "4"). It held 10,000–15,000 books on closed stacks.
- **Dining hall:** west wing, entered from Times Square. It seated over 250, at four-seat tables in later years.
  - **Tear-gas canisters** were fixed to the ceiling rafters and could be fired remotely from the gun gallery or outside posts; they were never used. One secondary source says there were 14; this is unverified.
  - The kitchen is beyond it to the west. The **bakery** was in the kitchen wing, and its vent pipe or smokestack runs up the exterior.
- **Hospital:** second floor of the west wing, above the dining hall, reached by the stairs at the NW corner of the cellhouse.

## 5. Recreation yard
- **Location:** off the west end, on the west side of D-block next to the dining wing, on a **raised level**. The yard door is at the SW corner of the cellhouse, up a short stair. From there a **wide, steep flight of concrete steps** goes down into the yard, and inmates used these steps as bleachers.
- **Size:** irregular, about **64 m** (210 ft, home plate to centre field). I estimate roughly **60 × 45 m**.
- **Surface:** concrete since 1936.
- **Walls:** concrete, about **6.1 m (20 ft)** high (1946 *Chronicle*), with fencing and barbed wire on top. Wikipedia says "25 ft", i.e. 7.6 m including the fence.
- **Guards:** an armed **catwalk** runs along the tops of the walls, and a gun post sits on the dining hall's exterior wall. Tower #3 is just to the west.

## 6. Roof and the June 1962 escape route
- **Roof:** low and flat, covered in tar and gravel, with long skylights and **several box ventilators** over the blocks. No source gives the number of ventilators; tool-proof grilles were fitted in 1937.
- **Route:**
  1. **Cells:** Morris B-138, West B-140, the Anglins B-150 and B-152. "Outside of B-block" means the Michigan Avenue side. They were probably on the ground tier, judging by the 1xx numbering.
  2. They dug out the vent grilles into the B-block utility corridor.
  3. They climbed about 9 m of pipes to the top of the block, where they had a workshop on the empty top tier.
  4. They went up the ventilator shaft **directly above B-block**. Its fan had been removed in 1957. They drilled out the rivets and bent a 30-cm (12-in) bar.
  5. They crossed the roof to its **north/west end**.
  6. They slid down the **kitchen/bakery vent pipe, about 15 m (50 ft)**.
  7. They climbed two **3.7 m (12 ft)** fences and launched from the **NE shore near the power plant**.

## 7. Colours and materials (low confidence; I found no NPS paint study)
- **Structure:** board-formed concrete. The exterior is painted or limewashed a cream/buff colour. Steel-sash windows with bars.
- **Floors:** bare grey concrete, smooth and worn.
- **Cells:** pale **"cucumber"/mint green with off-white**. One hobbyist matched this to the escapees' fake wall panels. Separately, an NPS collection record describes **green paint** used to disguise wall sections and vent grilles.
- **Bars:** painted a light colour (exact shade unknown).
- **D-block:** pink paint today, possibly later than 1962.
- **Lighting:** fluorescent fixtures plus daylight from the high windows and skylights.

# Alcatraz Island exterior, June 1962: reference for a 3D level

Positions come from OpenStreetMap building footprints, which I analysed myself (accurate to about ±5 m). The footprints are modern, but the surviving 1962 buildings stand on the same ground. Sizes and dates come from the NPS (National Register nomination and Cultural Landscapes Inventory) and Wikipedia. Figures marked **est.** are my own estimates.

## 1. Island geometry
- **Centre:** 37.8267 N, 122.4230 W. Cellhouse centre is about 37.8268 N, 122.4231 W.
- **Long axis:** bearing **125°/305°**, so the island runs NW–SE (more exactly WNW–ESE). The industrial end is NW; the parade ground is SE.
- **Size (sources disagree):**

| Source | Length | Width | Height | Area |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wikipedia (1971 documentary) | 1,675 ft = 511 m | 590 ft = 180 m | 135 ft = 41 m | — |
| NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory, original island | 1,705 ft = 520 m | 580 ft = 177 m | two peaks, 134.9/138.4 ft = 41.1/42.2 m | — |
| National Register nomination (1985) | about 1/3 mile ≈ 536 m | 525 ft = 160 m | 140 ft = 42.7 m | 22.5 acres = 9.1 ha |
| OpenStreetMap coastline today (includes riprap) | 541 m | 205 m | — | 8.0 ha |

- **Best estimate:** about **515 × 180 m**, maximum elevation **41 m**.
- **Summit:** the Army blasted it into a plateau about **40–41 m** high, sloping slightly from south to north. The mapped high point is about 48 m ESE of the cellhouse centre.

## 2. Coastline by side
The Army blasted the shore all round into a vertical rock face **7.6 m (25 ft)** high in 1853–54.
- **SW/W side (faces the Golden Gate):** the steepest. A 15 m vertical face rises from the water, then the ground climbs another 24 m within about 90 m. Total cliff is about **30 m (≈100 ft)**. A mid-level West Road runs along this side.
- **NW tip:** originally cliffs rose from 3 m to 37 m within 90 m. The 1962 industrial terraces sit here, with riprap (added 1935) and seawall. A 1935 landslide left the Model Industries Building 0.76 m from the cliff edge.
- **NE/E side (faces Berkeley):** a 6 m vertical face, then a steep slope to 40 m within 90 m. The **only practical landing** (the dock) is on this side.
- **SE end:** originally a gentle slope. The Army cut it down into the **parade ground terrace (≈18 m above sea level, from "60 ft")**, which has a sea wall and short cliffs below it.

## 4. Security features
**Guard towers.** There were six by 1940.
1. **Dock Tower**, E+113 N+37. Rebuilt about 1940 from a 1940 plan; it is the one that survives.
2. **Road Tower** on West Road, south/west of the yard (1934). This is possibly the "Tower No. 3" named near the yard.
3. **Hill Tower**, on the NW hillside, joined by a catwalk to the Model Industries tower.
4. **Model Industries roof tower** (June 1936). Octagonal cab with a pointed overhanging roof. Still on the roof in 1985; now displayed opposite the Power House.
5. **North Caponier / Power House tower** (1934, re-planned 1940).
6. **Cellhouse roof tower** at the north corner (about 1939–40). One secondary source says it had been **removed by 1962** and that budget cuts had taken several towers out of service. I could not confirm which towers were manned in June 1962.
- No tower heights were found. **Est.** cab floor 6–12 m above its base.

**Fences.** Chain-link with barbed wire on top, about **3.7 m (12 ft)** high (one source says 16 ft = 4.9 m). The run starts at the west-side incinerator, goes along the west edge to the Model Industries Building, along the north edge (enclosing the fog siren), past the shops and behind the Power House. Steel walkways let dog patrols pass the buildings. The dock area was also fenced.

**Brick defensive wall** along the switchback road: 6.1–7.6 m high with granite coping.

**Searchlights** were in the towers. The **NE shore by the Power House was a blind spot**, which is where the June 1962 escapees launched.

**Signs** stood at the NW (Model Industries), on the west road wall, at the SE end and at the dock. One read, in substance, "Only Government boats permitted within 200 yards" (183 m).

## 5. Roads and stairs
- **Main road:** from the dock it runs north to the Sally Port. It then climbs a Z with **three switchbacks**:
  - first switchback at the Post Exchange (E+2 N+91);
  - second at E+72 N+16, where a branch went south to the parade ground;
  - third at E−7 N+46;
  - the road ends at the SE side of the summit (E+70 N−20), by the lighthouse and Warden's House.
- **Climb:** about 400–500 m long (est.) and about 38 m of height, a grade of roughly 8–10%. Concrete surface.
- **East Road** continues NW along the shore to the Power House. The north and west roads were macadam.
- **Stairs:** a steep flight from the far (NW) corner of the yard down to the 1934 metal-detector hut and the industries area; stairs between the dock and the road; short flights round the lighthouse. Steps to the old quarry dock still appeared on a 1961 map.

## 6. Fog signals
- **Two**, one at each end: the **NW tip** (inside the industrial fence) and the **SE end** below the parade ground.
- Today's pattern is two 4-second blasts every 30 s at the SE and one 5-second blast every 20 s at the NW. I did not find the 1962 pattern.
- The light was still manned by keepers in 1962 and automated in 1963.

## 7. Surroundings and environment
**Distances (computed):**

| Yerba Buena Island | 5.1 km | 110° |
| Sausalito | 6.3 km | 303° |

- Accounts of the escape say Angel Island was "about 2 miles" (3.2 km) away.

**Water temperature:**
- NOAA San Francisco station June mean is **57.3°F = 14.1°C** (May 13.2°C, July 15.3°C).
- NPS gives the classic figure of 53°F = 11.7°C.
- One secondary source gives 54°F = 12.2°C on the night of 11 June 1962.
- **Use 12–14°C.**

**Currents:**
- Flood sets about 070° true (NOAA current station SFB1211, 0.5 mile north of the island); ebb runs W/SW toward the Golden Gate.
- **Est.** 0.5–1.5 m/s (1–3 kt) round the island. Over 3 m/s (6 kt) is quoted for the Gate.
- Back-eddies form on the east side during the flood.

**June nights:**
- Peak season for sea fog. The fog bank usually pushes in through the Golden Gate on afternoon westerlies (est. 15–25 kt) and often covers the island overnight, with foghorns running and occasional drizzle.
- Air about 11–13°C at night (est.). Clear nights also happen.
- I found no verified weather for 11 June 1962. Lights-out was 21:30 and the raft launched at about 22:00.

## 8. 1962 vs today
- **Remove** modern additions: Agave Trail, NPS restrooms, bookstore, ferry canopy, Occupation graffiti, ruins.
- **Restore** what has gone: the Warden's House, Post Exchange and lighthouse keepers' quarters intact; the parade-ground apartments; the towers; the barbed-wire fences.
